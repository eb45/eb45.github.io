PLM Research

Subsurface sea temperature

Deep learning reconstruction of sea subsurface temperature from surface climate fields.

The model takes sea surface temperature, sea surface height, zonal and meridional surface wind, plus latitude and longitude, and predicts temperature at depth. Fully connected LSTM layers are replaced with convolutional ones so the network keeps spatial structure: convolutions use local neighborhood and sparse connections instead of flattening the map.

Experiments include ConvLSTM variants with sigmoid/tanh and sigmoid/ELU activations, the same stacks with a land mask, and a hybrid CNN–LSTM. Training can be watched as a 2D map each epoch, since every sample is tied to latitude and longitude.

Pediatric CXR classification

CLIP-style domain adaptation from adult chest X-rays to pediatric pneumonia vs. normal.

An image–text model is pretrained with contrastive loss on adult NIH CXRs, then the image encoder is fine-tuned on the Kermany pediatric set. At test time the image is classified by nearest text prompt (Normal / Pneumonia). That pipeline is compared with a pediatric-only ResNet trained with cross-entropy, and with two other adaptation methods: domain-adversarial training (DANN) and optimal-transport Wasserstein (sliced Wasserstein distance).

Learning curves report AUC and F1 against the fraction of pediatric data; evaluation includes ROC, precision–recall, calibration, and Grad-CAM. ECE 685 final project.

AV TTC prediction

Time-to-collision as a risk score on nuScenes, using a frozen StreamPETR detector and a small MLP head.

Ground-truth TTC is built from a physics closure (distance over closing speed, capped at 10 s). A physics baseline runs that same formula on StreamPETR boxes and velocities. A learned head regresses seconds-to-collision from object query features, with and without explicit velocity embeddings, so the same detections yield both an interpretable baseline and a learned estimate.

On the trainval split, the physics baseline is strongest overall (MAE 1.48 s). Adding velocity to the MLP recovers most of the gap (MAE 1.71 s vs 3.57 s for query features alone). Short-horizon bins stay hard: nuScenes has almost no sub-second collisions, so the learned head has little supervision there. Built on StreamPETR (ICCV 2023).
